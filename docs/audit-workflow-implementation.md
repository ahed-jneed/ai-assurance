> **Superseded for status (14 Nov 2026).** This is a dated record of an earlier increment, kept as written. ## What auditors can complete

New engagements start with draft program candidates and no precreated completed-looking papers. Existing engagements adopt explicitly; signed content and reports are preserved. An independently approved exclusion affects whether its linked legacy paper is required for this engagement, without rewriting that paper. Limitation memos remain required and must be evaluated, reviewed and signed. Reopening the evidence request retires its memo (it then counts towards no gate or snapshot), and closing the request again revises the same memo as a new revision instead of adding a second one. An applicable procedure omitted from audit scope prevents an unmodified or readiness conclusion; qualified, adverse and disclaimer reports remain subject to documented completion gates.

Lifecycle gates are stage-scoped (`lib/domain/gates.ts`): entering a stage needs only the work of the stages before it. Entering Fieldwork needs the approved classification, scope and program and the selected Planning procedures and papers and, for structured engagements, the approved acceptance record (its period agreeing with the brief) and a current, conflict-free declaration from every team member (`lib/workflow/acceptance.ts`). The team is the people the acceptance record names plus the preparers and reviewers of selected procedures; a declaration counts for the accepted period only, and is outdated once someone outside the team it was made for joins. Workflows adopted before the gate (no `acceptanceRequired` flag and no acceptance record) and engagements already past Planning are not gated retroactively; the Acceptance tab prompts them to complete the record. The report's independence element (ISAE 3000 report elements, `lib/domain/report-gates.ts`) follows the same rule at final capture: where the gate applies it needs the approved acceptance record and current, conflict-free declarations, and its gate opens the Acceptance tab; an older workflow keeps the brief's free-text assessment. Entering Evidence evaluation needs completed diagnostic runs; entering Reporting needs the Planning, Fieldwork and Evidence evaluation work; entering Sign-off additionally needs the Reporting work (WP-RP-01). WP-RP-02 and MEM-SO-01 are release-time records: their evidence is the final package itself, so no gate or progress count asks for their papers or procedures, and the procedure and paper commands refuse to execute, prepare, review or sign them (409); the audit program and paper register label them "Recorded at release". Final capture needs every other stage's work; the release records them in `release.completion`, bound to the two package hashes the independent approval covers. Reporting therefore exits when its own work is signed, and Sign-off when the captured package is approved and released.

New work on a procedure whose compiled paper is signed compiles a new paper and supersedes the signed one (`supersededBy`, `required: false`); a signed legacy paper an adopted procedure took over is superseded the same way. A superseded paper stays as it was signed and refuses every paper command. Gates, the paper register, the Annex IV index and report snapshots count only current papers; an internal snapshot lists superseded papers separately under `supersededPapers`, and retired limitation memos under `retiredPapers`, so `evidenceRequests[].limitationPaperId` always resolves. The Annex IV index matches a mapped template's current signed or required papers, ignores unsigned supplementary drafts such as automatic diagnostic papers, and records an explicit gap for a mapped template without a current paper.

Only one final report is released at a time. Releasing a report supersedes every report released before it (the named predecessor, or the latest when none is named) and records a client notification in the release history. The client projection names the reports a released report replaced (`replaces`), and the client report list, report record and print view show it. A superseded report stays readable to the client, which was issued that opinion, labelled "Superseded by REPORT-…" (`supersededBy`; decided 6 Nov 2026); its client package and sealed client dossier stay downloadable.

### Report model v2 (#20)

A report states its terms as data (`lib/domain/report-model.ts`): the engagement type (attestation, direct, certification audit, agreed-upon procedures, readiness assessment), the assurance level (limited or reasonable; not applicable to agreed-upon procedures and readiness assessments) and the conclusion (unmodified, qualified, adverse or disclaimer for assurance engagements; factual findings for agreed-upon procedures; a readiness assessment for readiness engagements). Reasonable assurance is worded as an opinion ("In our opinion, …"), limited assurance as a negative-form conclusion ("… nothing has come to our attention …"); qualified, adverse and disclaimer reports carry a basis-for-modification section, and agreed-upon procedures and readiness reports are labelled as not assurance. The wording is our own drafting around the conventional phrases; no standard text is reproduced (D-03).

Unmodified and readiness conclusions are positive: they need every in-scope conclusion supported, no open High or Critical finding, no unobtainable evidence and no prohibited practice. Adverse is for material and pervasive deficiencies; a disclaimer for a pervasive inability to obtain evidence. Final capture also checks the ISAE 3000 (Revised) report elements (para. 69, `lib/domain/report-gates.ts`): title, addressee, subject matter and criteria, responsibilities, the standard applied, the independence and quality-management statement, the summary of work, the conclusion (with its basis when modified), signature, date and location. The subject matter, criteria, responsibilities and independence come from the approved brief, the summary of work from the program; the Lead Auditor states the addressee, signatory, location and basis when preparing the report. Results roll up by criterion (the procedures' `criterion`) with their outcomes and open findings.

Snapshots of report model v2 have `contractVersion` "2.0" and record `terms`, `particulars`, `reportTitle`, `elements`, `conclusionStatement` and `criteria` beside `opinion`, which holds the label (e.g. "Qualified conclusion · limited assurance"). Reports captured before v2 keep their `opinion` string ("Readiness assessment", "Qualified conclusion", "Adverse conclusion", "Unable to conclude"); they render as issued and are rechecked on release with that opinion, without report-element gates.

One client projection (`lib/client-projection.ts`, F-57) builds both the live client view and the client snapshot of a report from the same per-record functions: the classification without rationale, intended-purpose notes or approver, evidence without storage keys or uploader ids, findings without internal fields. A released report publishes the conclusions of current signed papers whose audience is client; memos default to internal, working papers to client, and a Lead Auditor can change a paper's audience (`setPaperAudience`) under Reports → Completion checklist → Client report content. A v2 snapshot records each paper's audience, so changing one after capture makes the package stale. A follow-up case decided as needing no reassessment needs accepted evidence when it is assessed; other decisions may be recorded first, and closure needs accepted evidence except for a reassessment, which the linked successor engagement carries.

### Engagement quality review (#24)

The Engagement Quality Reviewer is a membership role (ISQM 2), granted, changed and revoked through the practice's member management like every role, so each change is a management event. The reviewer reads the engagement and records only the quality review (`lib/domain/quality-review.ts`, `lib/commands/quality-review.ts`): every other engagement command refuses the role before its own checks run (`refuseQualityReviewer` in `lib/tenancy.ts`, applied by the command dispatcher and again by `save`), so a reviewer never prepares, edits, reviews or signs papers, procedures or any other audit work.

- **Appointment.** A Lead Auditor appoints the reviewer for an engagement (`appointQualityReviewer`, with a reason) under Reports & sign-off → Quality review. The appointee must be an active member with the role and access to the engagement, must not be on the engagement team (anyone the acceptance record names, anyone who prepared, reviewed, approved or led any of its recorded work: brief, program, procedures, papers, findings, evidence requests, classification, reports and releases, and anyone who held access to the engagement under any role other than Engagement Quality Reviewer, even if that grant was later withdrawn) and must have cooled off from the team of the client's other engagements: the period, the practice's quality review policy (two years by default, ISQM 2 ¶19), runs from their latest recorded involvement there. A Lead Auditor who administers the practice sets the period under Members & access → Quality review policy (`setQualityReviewPolicy`, with a reason, recorded as a management event with the old and new period). At release the appointed reviewer must still be an active member with the Engagement Quality Reviewer role. The appointment records the period it applied. Replacing a reviewer starts a fresh checklist; earlier sign-offs stay as history and cover nothing for the new reviewer.
- **Stand-back checklist.** Seven items in our own words, each tied to ISQM 2 ¶25 (D-03): understanding the engagement, significant matters and judgments, the papers behind them, the independence determination, consultation, the Lead's involvement and the report. Each item is satisfied (with notes) or an open concern. Only the appointed reviewer saves it, and each save is a new revision.
- **Gates.** An assurance engagement type (attestation, direct engagement, certification audit) always needs the review; agreed-upon procedures and readiness assessments need it only when a Lead has appointed a reviewer under firm policy. Final capture needs an appointed reviewer outside the team and a complete checklist (`qualityReviewGates`, part of `finalReportGates`). Release additionally needs the reviewer's sign-off (`signOffQualityReview`) bound to both exact package hashes and to the current checklist revision (`qualityReviewReleaseProblem`, checked by `releaseReport`); a checklist saved after the sign-off needs a new one, and a reviewer who has since joined the team is refused.
- **Confidentiality.** Quality review records stay internal: the client projection and client snapshots never include them.

Earlier final snapshots without a release history remain available internally. They are not automatically represented as independently approved or client-released. A new issuance must capture the current approved program and completed review packet; release cannot retrofit those facts into a legacy snapshot.

## Common execution and technical controls

`lib/workflow-types.ts` is the application contract. `schemas/procedure-execution.schema.json` describes the portable actual-execution record; `schemas/test-run.schema.json` extends hosted run 1.1 with exact procedure/scope bindings and retry lineage.

A procedure plan has a substantive part (selection, applicability, rationale, criterion, source version, period analysis, population, sampling, method and pinned inputs, with the methodology objective and steps) and an assignment (preparer, reviewer and due date). The plan revision and a SHA-256 plan hash cover the substantive part only; the assignment has its own revision. An execution binds the plan hash and the brief revision, so it stays current until its own procedure's substantive plan or the scope changes: changes to other procedures, and the program revisions they create, never invalidate it. A substantive change returns an approved program to draft and invalidates that procedure's execution and review only; reviewed work goes back for review, so restoring its plan makes the execution current again but not the review. An assignment change keeps the program approval and recorded work; reviewed work keeps its reviewer, and approved work keeps a preparer, reviewer and deadline. Executions recorded before plan hashes keep their plan-revision binding. Because the plan form carries the assignment, a plan save names the assignment revision it was opened at and is refused with 409 if the preparer, reviewer or due date changed since, instead of reverting that change. A saved recovery draft of the plan form is dated by the assignment revision as well as the plan, execution, program and scope revisions, so after a reassignment it is offered only for export and comparison, not restored; execution and evaluation drafts, which hold no assignment, stay restorable.

Histories keep submissions, not drafting. A brief, program or procedure plan is amended until its next submission. The program history records each submission and its outcome: approval, return with the reviewer's reason, or withdrawal when a Lead's plan or scope change takes it back to draft. The brief history keeps each returned revision and each approved revision a Lead revises. A procedure keeps its plan as submitted at its first change after a submission, a review that a plan change clears, and its state before each execution, evaluation, impact review and return, each entry marked with the change that followed it. A brief return stores `returnReason`, `returnedBy` and `returnedAt` instead of rewriting the preparer's change impact.

History limits never stop a change halfway. The scope and the program count returns only, 40 and 75: a submission that is approved or withdrawn uses none, so an approved scope or program always has a submission left, and a plan change or scope change made to it can always be submitted and approved again. Returns are never refused; a scope or program returned to its limit can still be drafted but not submitted again. A change that would need a submission the limit refuses, possible only if a limit is lowered, is refused before it lands, and the approved records stay in force. A procedure counts 30 work revisions (executions, evaluations and impact reviews; plan changes and returns use none), and at its limit only redoing current work is refused: the first execution and evaluation, new work or an impact review after a plan or scope change made the work stale, plan changes and reassignments are always accepted. The audit program warns five returns or work revisions before a limit.

An entry's shape, never its date, decides whether it counts. Every procedure history entry the commands write records its `action`; an entry without one was written before actions existed and counts towards no limit, whenever it was written, so a procedure that reached the earlier 30-entry limit can be reassigned, replanned and worked on again. Likewise, returned brief revisions without a `returnReason` and older program entries, none of which is a "returned" entry, are not counted.

Approval cycles and Lead withdrawals count towards no history limit; the engagement's 1.5 MB metadata limit bounds them. For a program of 33 procedures, each submission and each approval or withdrawal adds an entry of about 4 KB that binds every procedure's plan revision and hash, so a cycle adds about 8 KB: roughly 170 cycles fit in an otherwise small engagement, fewer as its other records grow. `save()` in `lib/engagement-repo.ts` measures the stored (dehydrated) engagement before the single D1 batch that writes the engagement, its snapshot and its audit event, and refuses with 413 before anything is written:

- A write that would take the record over 1.5 MB: "This change would take the engagement record over its 1.5 MB limit. Nothing was saved. Shorten the text you are adding, or continue in a follow-up engagement."
- So that the limit never stops an approval halfway, 100 KB under it are kept for approving the scope and program (`metadataReserve()` in `lib/workflow-state.ts`). A write that starts an approval (adopting the workflow, or taking an approved scope, an approved program or a submitted program back to draft) must leave all 100 KB free, or it is refused with "This change would need the engagement scope or audit program to be approved, but would leave less than the 100 KB that approval needs …". The approved scope, program and plans then stay in force exactly as they were, and changes that need no approval (such as a preparer, reviewer or due date) still go through.
- A submission of the scope or program must leave room for the reviewer to approve or return it (the submitted record plus two 2,000-character reasons).
- Once a cycle has started, its own writes may use the reserve: drafting the brief and plans, and the reviewer's approvals and returns. While the scope or program awaits approval, any other write may not grow the record into the reserve (or into the room kept for a pending decision, if larger). A Lead's change that would withdraw a submission inside the reserve is refused, since it would start another cycle; the reviewer can still return or approve it.

From 200 KB before the limit, the audit program warns how much of it is used and what the reserve refuses (`metadataWarning()`; the workspace view passes the stored size to internal roles). Content-addressed R2 objects that a refused write had already stored stay unreferenced, as after a lost version check. Further approval cycles then belong in a follow-up engagement.

An approved automated/mixed procedure pins the registered model version, accepted prediction dataset and immutable evaluation protocol. Queueing rejects mismatched pins. Each run carries a SHA-256 of its procedure/brief/program binding alongside existing manifest, configuration, model and dataset hashes. Execution verifies frozen bindings and input bytes. Compilation accepts only succeeded runs bound to the procedure's current plan revision and the current brief revision, with the planned inputs; a run queued under an earlier program revision still counts. A technical outcome never substitutes for the auditor's evidence evaluation.

Failed/cancelled evaluations can be queued as a new retry after input integrity and current scope are revalidated. The prior result is preserved and `retryOf` records lineage. Interactive jobs use a guarded queued → running claim and guarded completion; concurrent edits do not silently overwrite state. Recovery is explicit for interrupted claims. Hosted execution remains bounded saved-prediction diagnostics, not a separately provisioned native-model worker fleet.

`/api/report-package` verifies the captured report's SHA-256 and returns a deterministic reference manifest of captured paper hashes and evidence pins. Proof 1.1 (#137) also lists every exported working-paper contract document of an internal package, each with the SHA-256 of the bytes the dossier files under `papers/`: documents captured in the snapshot are covered by its digest, and the completion memo (MEM-SO-01), which is built from the release record, counts as valid only when its approvals and evidence bind the package's hashes. The verification panel lists these documents. Authorized users can download the corresponding verifiable package. Client users receive only the client projection of a released report, or of one a later release superseded. Withdrawal removes access to that projection while preserving internal snapshot bytes and both digests. Verification does not assert external timestamping, qualified signatures, or that every R2 object was reread.

Every mutation uses engagement/management version checks and same-origin enforcement. Winning D1 commands append a snapshot and audit event atomically. The membership guard rechecks active role **and client assignment**, preventing a same-role reassignment from authorizing an old client upload. Grant revocation changes the engagement version; losing file writes are cleaned up. Client projections use explicit allowlists and do not include internal brief/program/procedure records, user directories, audit events or internal request descriptions.

Release rejects source records changed after capture, including procedure executions, reviewed papers, requests, findings, classification, the evidence manifest, model metadata, evaluation profiles and diagnostic runs. Prior approvals are tied to exact captured digests. Scope/program approval is independent of cumulative preparers; requesting corrections does not make the reviewer a preparer. Procedure review is restricted to its assigned independent reviewer; paper sign-off remains separate.

## Validation and operating boundaries

The integration fixture runs against ephemeral Miniflare D1/R2 storage, with fictional identities and synthetic evidence. It exercises the full journey, negative permissions, concurrent management commands, role revocation, exact input pins, compiled-paper boundaries, partial receipts, report release/withdrawal/projection, package verification, finding retest and linked successor creation. No production audit records or access grants are created for QA.

The current hosted aggregate has a 1.5 MB per-engagement limit and bounded histories. The normalized production architecture and separate Python execution service remain documented separately. Native binary adapters, isolated asynchronous external workers, SQL credentials, automated monitoring schedules and external notifications are not newly deployed by this increment. Monitoring recurrence is an explicit planning action. Current legal applicability, evidence sufficiency and assurance opinion require competent human decisions using the recorded regulatory source and audit-period analysis.

## Business workspace usability

The personal work inbox derives actions from procedures and their dependencies across authorized engagements, with server-stored views and reminder preferences. Procedure workspaces connect accepted evidence, client requests, sampling records, current diagnostic runs, findings, and saved review comparisons. Private recoverable drafts remain separate from audit revisions and require explicit saving; stale drafts cannot overwrite a changed audit basis.

Member onboarding combines role selection, engagement assignments, and a review step. Client request packages support required-item checklists, multiple files, clarification threads, and separate receipt acknowledgment and evidence acceptance. Site access remains a separate host permission. Reminders are in-app; no email delivery is configured.

Dataset cards are immutable versions tied to accepted artifact hashes and optional baseline pins. Testing readiness evaluates the exact procedure, protocol, input, execution mode, and native execution approvals before queue handoff. Bulk planning checks assignments and reviewer independence before revising a program. Permission-checked links and contextual text previews preserve engagement access boundaries.

Shared form grids align labels and controls consistently, including native date fields, wrapped labels, and optional field guidance. Mobile forms stack and use 44px controls.
